## Question

You are a power systems engineer preparing a cell-chemistry trade study for a Shuttle payload experiment. The experiment requires a primary cell that simultaneously satisfies all three of the following: (1) a maximum energy density of at least 300 Wh/kg, (2) an operating temperature range that includes -40°C, and (3) a NASA-recommended shelf life of at least 2 years at room temperature. Using Table 3-1 (Primary Battery Cell Characteristics) and its footnotes, identify every electrochemical couple that meets all three criteria and list each qualifying couple's nominal cell load voltage at room temperature. Then cross-reference Table 5-4 (Energy Density of D-Size Cells) to state which qualifying couple appears in that D-size comparison and give its Wh/kg and Wh/liter values. Finally, state the footnote caveat in Table 3-1 that limits how the tabulated energy densities should be interpreted.

## Gold answer

Four couples in Table 3-1 meet all three criteria: lithium-thionyl chloride (330 Wh/kg; -40 to 70 deg C; 2 yr or more at room temp; 3.3 V), lithium-thionyl chloride/bromine chloride complex Li-BCX (275-350 Wh/kg; -40 to 70 deg C; 2 yr or more; 3.2 V), lithium-sulfur dioxide (180-355 Wh/kg; -40 to 60 deg C; 5 yr or more; 2.8 V), and lithium-polycarbon monofluoride (125-625 Wh/kg; -50 to 70 deg C; 5 yr or more; 2.7 V). Zinc-air alkaline (330 Wh/kg) is excluded because its operating range is only 0 to 45 deg C and its shelf life is only 1 to 1-1/2 yr; lithium-iron disulfide is excluded (maximum 173 Wh/kg, and the footnote restricts it to AA cells only). Cross-referencing Table 5-4, Li-BCX is the only qualifying couple represented in the D-size comparison, at 350 Wh/kg and 950 Wh/liter. Per the Table 3-1 footnote, the tabulated energy densities are based on the smaller sizes (less than 40 Ah) normally used on Shuttle applications, with the exception of the silver oxide-zinc cell.
